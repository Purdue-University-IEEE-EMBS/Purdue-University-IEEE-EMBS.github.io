## **Research and Biology Onboarding**

---

### **Role Overview**
The Research / Biology team ensures that all EMBS projects are **scientifically valid, medically relevant, and ethically responsible**. You act as the “bridge” between engineering and medicine: grounding technical builds in biology, physiology, and clinical context.

You don’t need to be a biology major, but you’ll learn how to **navigate medical research, interpret biosignals, and ensure ethical safety** in our student prototypes. This role prepares you for graduate research, industry R&D, or publishing with faculty mentors.

***

### **Why This Role Matters**
- Keeps projects grounded in science, not just circuits.
- Ensures ethical responsibility and student safety.
- Helps EMBS publish & present work that earns credibility.
- Connects projects to faculty mentors → potential for IRB studies, funding, and real-world impact.

***

### **Responsibilities**
- Provide medical context for engineering teams (e.g., “What does this biosignal mean?”).
- Perform literature reviews (PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar).
- Investigate methods & benchmarks (how real medical devices are tested).
- Support ethical compliance (understand IRB, HIPAA, Purdue safety rules).
- Assist with proposals (help teams generate credible research questions).
- Write abstracts, reports, and posters for project deliverables and conferences.
- Engage with faculty (seek advisors, identify potential PIs, or confirm feasibility).

***

### **Onboarding Process**
1. **Learn** → Study physiology, ethics, and literature review.
2. **Learning Check** → Complete the learning check.
3. **Assignments** → Submit article reviews and ethics considerations
4. **Review** → Research lead checks submissions.
5. **Discord Update** → Unlock “Research / Biology” role.

***

### **Skills to Learn**
**Physiology Basics**
- Heart rate, breathing, oxygen saturation (SpO₂).
- Biosignals: ECG, EMG, EEG, PPG.
- Audio diagnostics: heart sounds, lung sounds.

**Literature Review**
- How to search PubMed & IEEE Xplore.
- How to break down a paper (abstract → methods → results → limitations).
- How to summarize findings for engineers.

**Research Ethics & Safety**
- HIPAA basics (privacy & de-identification).
- IRB process (when human subject work requires approval).
- Purdue safety forms & lab rules.
- Non-invasive data collection protocols.

**Benchmarking & Validation**
- Clinical vs. hobbyist accuracy (what’s “good enough” for a demo vs. FDA-level).
- Compare your team’s device against published standards.

**Proposal Writing**
- Framing a research question.
- Generating hypotheses.
- Drafting methods sections.
- Writing clear abstracts and posters.

### **Play-by-Play Research Path**
1. Paper Review / Covidance
    - Learn to read and critique a scientific paper.
    - Practice identifying the abstract, methods, and key findings.
2. Literature Review
    - Collect papers on your project topic (e.g., sleep apnea detection).
    - Organize findings into background & methods summaries.
3. Research Question Generation
    - Use gaps in literature to propose a testable idea.
    - Example: “Can we use a microphone + SpO₂ sensor to detect apnea events?”
4. Proposal Drafting
    - Write a structured proposal: background, methods, goals, expected results.
    - Share with your team and faculty mentor.
5. Faculty Advisor / PI Engagement
    - Present your idea to professors.
    - Ask if they’d be willing to act as PI (Principal Investigator) or advisor.
    - Strong research ideas are more likely to secure mentorship.
6. Ethics & IRB
    - If human subjects are involved → IRB approval is mandatory.
    - IRB requires a faculty PI and Purdue Human Research Protection Program forms.
    - If IRB is too ambitious → consider a Scoping Review (systematic review of published articles, no new subjects).
7. Experiment / Data Collection
    - Run safe, small-scale tests (non-invasive sensors only).
    - Follow Purdue safety guidelines.
8. Abstract & Poster
    - Summarize findings into a short abstract.
    - Build a research poster for EMBS showcase or a conference.
